Origin

I started playing Metal Gear on the NES in 1993. Around the same time I was watching Escape from New York, Blade Runner, The Matrix, and anything else with that dark, noir, science-fiction future.

Computers talking back. Artificial intelligence. HUDs. Terminals. Machines with personalities. Cyberdecks.

I always wanted my own. Most of that was fiction then. The idea never left.

My background is engineering, not “software engineer first.” Programming (VBA, Python, C) was a tool. Around 2018 I got serious about ML and AI — XGBoost, TensorFlow, predictive maintenance — and AI stopped being something I only saw in games.

Building the thing I always wanted

OtaconsKeep is roughly three years of building, breaking, and rebuilding around one question:

What would the computer I always wanted look like if I could build it today?

Not another chatbot. A system: agents with roles and personalities, persistent memory, voice, automation, tools — something meaningful on the other end of the Codec.

That became OtaconsKeep: local models, memory, routing, home-lab infrastructure, and agents that work together. I don’t just want impressive demos. I want to know it works — then prove it. The how lives in Engineering. This page is the why.

Why make it public?

For a long time this was only my Keep. Then I realized other people might want their Keep — their agents, hardware, and automations — not a clone of mine.

So I turned it into something people can install. I’m documenting the broken installers and failed experiments too. That’s part of building it.

Still building the cyberdeck

Consumer GPUs, open source, and a home lab can now do things that seemed ridiculous when I was a kid.

More than thirty years after that NES, I’m still chasing the same idea.

I still want my cyberdeck. The difference is that now I can actually build it.

Welcome to the Keep.
